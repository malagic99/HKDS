The Concept

A third path between guns and missiles

Current air defense doctrine relies on expensive guided interceptors (Patriot PAC-3 at $4M per round, Iron Dome at $50–100k per intercept) or conventional kinetic systems whose velocity and fire-control latency are inadequate against modern hypersonic threats. HKDS proposes a third category — hypervelocity kinetic rounds at $3–8 per shot, guided by sub-millisecond photonic fire control.

System overview

Distributed, autonomous, defensive battery

HKDS surrounds a defended site with corner-mounted turret nodes and a 10+ km layered sensor perimeter. A central Fiber Loop Photonics (FLP) node ingests radar, optical, acoustic and fiber-Bragg sensor data, computes lead solutions and maneuver envelopes via photonic reservoir computing, and pushes firing commands to the turrets in under a millisecond.

  • Fixed-site only

    Installed as a permanent or semi-permanent area-denial shield around bases and critical assets.

  • Multi-threat coverage

    FPV drones, loitering munitions, cruise missiles, hypersonic glide vehicles, terminal ballistic reentry.

  • Photonic intelligence core

    FLP fire control processes time-series sensor data and issues firing decisions entirely in the optical domain.

Design drivers

Three constraints that shape every design choice

HKDS is driven by the intersection of three demands no current system meets simultaneously. Each pillar of the architecture is the answer to one of them.

Velocity
Match the threat
Projectile speed must be competitive with hypersonic terminal velocities to keep lead distances manageable.
Latency
Decide in ms
Fire control must compute valid solutions within seconds of warning, not tens of seconds.
Cost
Fight saturation
Cost per engagement must stay low enough to withstand mixed-salvo attacks.

Lead distance = v_target × (range / v_projectile). At 5,000 m/s, the HKDS round is 5–7× more forgiving on lead than a Phalanx CIWS round. Combined with sub-millisecond fire control, lead error effectively disappears as a miss contributor at close range.

Three technology pillars

Velocity. Coverage. Latency.

Each HKDS pillar individually exists in adjacent fields. Light gas guns are established research tools; belt-fed weapons are mature; FBG sensing is space-qualified; photonic reservoir computing has been demonstrated end-to-end by Duport et al. (2012), Duport et al. (2016) and Wang et al. (2025). The HKDS engineering challenge is integration, not invention.

Why now

Three trends converge.

Hypersonic threats have moved from frontier research to active deployment; missile-based air defense scales linearly in cost while threats scale exponentially in volume; and the photonic substrate that makes sub-millisecond fire control viable has just crossed the line from laboratory demo to telecom-grade supply chain.

Hypersonic deployment is here, not coming.

HGVs and maneuvering reentry vehicles are now fielded by multiple nation-state actors. No deployed Western kinetic system engages them at competitive velocity.

Missile-based defense doesn't scale.

$3–4M Patriot rounds against $20k loitering munitions is a losing economic war. Cost asymmetry is the structural problem — and it inverts when intercept material drops below $10.

Photonic fire control is manufacturable.

All FLP components are telecom-qualified or space-qualified today. A sovereign-EU supply chain (Exail, LioniX, TOPTICA, Thales Alenia, ST) covers the full stack — ITAR-free.

Lead calculation

Hypervelocity collapses lead error

Lead distance scales linearly with target speed and inversely with projectile speed. A faster round means less lead, and less sensitivity to whatever the target does between fire command and intercept.

Operational regimes

Three regimes, one system

  • Lower-tier (FPV, loitering)

    Tens to hundreds of seconds of warning. Engaged primarily by conventional weapons routed through FLP, preserving hypervelocity capacity.

  • Cruise missiles

    Warning compresses to 10–40 s. HKDS combines hypervelocity rounds with photonic lead calculation to keep intercept windows open.

  • Hypersonic & ballistic terminal

    1.4–6.7 s windows. FLP delivers thousands of decision cycles inside the terminal trajectory.
