Scientific foundation

FLP fire control is built on published, peer-reviewed photonics.

HKDS does not require new physics. The photonic reservoir computer at the core of FLP fire control has been demonstrated in fiber by independent academic groups — including, critically, on the exact computational task HKDS performs: predicting a radar signal one to ten time steps into the future from a real backscatter dataset (Duport et al., Scientific Reports, 2016).

Headline result

In 2016, a fiber-loop photonic reservoir computer predicted real radar backscatter signals one to ten time steps ahead — with no digital pre- or post-processing in the loop.

The Duport et al. (2016) experiment used the IPIX radar dataset (sea-surface backscatter from McMaster University) as the input task. The fully analogue photonic reservoir matched the established literature on this task — proving the FLP-class architecture can perform the central computation that HKDS fire control performs against an incoming hypersonic target: take a noisy radar signal, predict its near-future evolution, and emit a continuous analogue output.

This is not a future capability. The benchmark task in the published paper is structurally identical to the HKDS fire-control computation. The remaining engineering effort is defense-grade packaging, vibration / EMI hardening and multi-target scaling — not novel physics. The ALTA spacecraft qualification program covers the hardening work in parallel.

Paper 01 — direct proof for HKDS

Duport, Smerieri, Akrout, Haelterman, Massar (2016)

Fully analogue photonic reservoir computer. Scientific Reports 6:22381 · DOI 10.1038/srep22381 · Université Libre de Bruxelles.

What the paper demonstrates

The first reservoir computer in which both the input layer and the output layer are fully analogue. Prior implementations always required digital pre-processing of the input or digital post-processing of the output — limiting effective throughput. The 2016 paper eliminates the A/D conversion bottleneck entirely.

  • Analogue optical input

    Input mask generated as a sum of two sinusoids — implementable with simple oscillators in future on-chip hardware.

  • Analogue electrical output

    A continuous analogue readout produces the time-series prediction directly — no ADC, no DSP pipeline.

  • Fiber-loop optoelectronic reservoir

    47 internal variables (N=47, k=5). Mach-Zehnder modulators with 10 GHz bandwidth. DFB telecom laser source. Off-the-shelf components.

  • Standalone operation

    The only role of the external computer is to compute the readout weight function w(t) once during training. Operation is autonomous.

The three benchmark tasks

The paper validates the architecture on three standard reservoir-computing benchmarks. The third — radar signal forecasting — maps directly onto the HKDS fire-control task.

Radar task in detail

IPIX sea-clutter backscatter

The radar dataset is taken from the McMaster University IPIX radar — real ocean-surface backscatter recorded at low sea state (0.8 m average wave height, max 1.3 m). The input is a two-dimensional signal: the I and Q channels of the demodulator output.

The reservoir is trained over 1,000 inputs and tested over a separate 1,000 inputs. For each of t+1 through t+10 time-step predictions, the all-analogue reservoir matches the all-optical reservoir baseline of an earlier paper (with similar internal variable count) and remains close to the numerical / digital reference.

Source: Duport et al. (2016), Fig. 8 — "Radar signal forecasting error (NMSE) with respect to the number of time steps of the prediction."

Paper 02 — feasibility

Duport, Schneider, Smerieri, Haelterman, Massar (2012)

All-optical reservoir computing. Optics Express 20(20):22783 · The first practical demonstration of all-optical reservoir computing using off-the-shelf fiber telecom components.

What this proved

Parity with state-of-the-art digital reservoir-computing implementations using nothing more than standard telecom-grade fiber components: SOAs, modulators, fiber loops. No exotic materials, no bespoke fabrication, no laboratory-only ingredients.

This established that photonic reservoir computing is a manufacturable technology, not a physics curiosity — the supply chain already exists.

Why HKDS cares

Defense-grade qualification is dramatically easier when the underlying components are already qualified to telecom and aerospace standards. Every component class used in the FLP fire-control chain — FBGs, DFB lasers, MZ modulators, balanced photodiodes — has a space-qualified or telecom-qualified pedigree.

The ALTA EU supply chain (Exail, LioniX, TOPTICA, Thales Alenia Space, ST) covers every component class in this stack — sovereign, ITAR-free, and exportable to U.S. defense primes subject only to EU dual-use controls.

Paper 03 — scaling

Wang et al. (2025)

Optical next-generation reservoir computing. Nature Photonics · Demonstrates O(1) energy scaling and high-accuracy chaotic time-series forecasting.

Energy scaling

O(1) vs O(N²) digital

The energy cost of a photonic reservoir computation is essentially independent of reservoir size, while digital systems scale as O(N²) with the number of internal states. For a saturated multi-target engagement, this is the difference between a tractable on-prem compute budget and a server-room thermal problem.

Accuracy

NRMSE 0.0971

On chaotic time-series forecasting — a harder benchmark than NARMA10 — the photonic reservoir achieves NRMSE of 0.0971 with 10× less training data than digital counterparts. The architecture is not a "lossy approximation" of digital; it is competitive.

Throughput

> 100 TOPS/mm²

Integrated photonic accelerators have exceeded 100 TOPS per square millimetre at tens of gigabaud. Against that backdrop, HKDS's < 1 ms fire-control budget is conservative, not optimistic — the compute density is already there.

Bottom line

HKDS fire control is an integration program — not a physics program.

The Duport 2016 paper is the single most consequential piece of evidence for HKDS's feasibility. It demonstrates that the exact computational pattern at the heart of fire control — predicting a noisy radar signal's near future — can be performed end-to-end in a fiber-optic photonic medium with no digital pipeline. The remaining work is defense-grade qualification, which the parallel ALTA spacecraft program addresses directly.
