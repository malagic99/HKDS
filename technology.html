Pillar 01 — Velocity

Helium acoustic velocity sets the ceiling

A light gas gun breaks past the chemical-propellant velocity ceiling by using a low molecular-weight gas as the accelerant. The fundamental ceiling of any gun is the acoustic velocity of the propellant gas — a projectile cannot outrun the pressure wave driving it.

Two-stage architecture

Electrically-driven piston compresses helium

In the HKDS belt-fed configuration the piston is driven electrically rather than by a powder charge. An electromagnetic piston driver compresses the pre-loaded helium capsule into the launch tube at a controlled rate — yielding repeatable muzzle velocity within ±0.5 % per shot.

  • Repeatable to ±0.5 %

    Critical for firing solution accuracy across the simultaneous-fire star turret.

  • Electrical reset 1.5–3 s

    Drives cyclic rate of 20–40 rpm per single barrel; 80–160 rpm aggregated across a 4-node battery.

  • No powder, no fouling

    Combustion products removed entirely — eliminates the logistics tail of propellant storage and barrel fouling cycles.

Propellant selection

Helium: safe, inert, fast enough

Acoustic velocity sets the ceiling on any gas-driven projectile. Helium offers enough acoustic velocity (970 m/s) to reach the 4–6 km/s regime while remaining inert, non-flammable, non-embrittling and well-understood in high-pressure systems.

Ammunition

Belt-fed rounds with on-board helium charge

Each HKDS round is a self-contained unit: a pre-pressurized helium capsule, a sacrificial polymer sabot, and a dense tungsten penetrator. No explosives, no electronics in the firing chain — a passive mechanical inertial trigger releases the helium charge only when chambered.

Round cross-section

Capsule → sabot → penetrator

The burst disk is scored to fail at a specific differential pressure. During transport and belt feeding, the capsule sees uniform pressure on both faces and the disk holds. The moment the chambering pin pierces the facing side, the differential drives rupture and helium releases into the pump stage — a purely mechanical, passive firing chain.

Kinetic energy

No warhead, no seeker — just KE

At hypervelocity, kinetic energy alone guarantees destruction. A 5 g tungsten rod at 5,000 m/s delivers more energy than a Hellfire warhead. Near-miss within 0.3–0.5 m produces a shockwave sufficient to defeat control surfaces, sensors and propulsion on any current threat platform.

Pillar 02 — Coverage

Replace precision with geometric coverage

Against an HGV maneuvering at 5 g, the target can move up to 70 m from its predicted position during a 1.5 s flight time. No fire-control system, regardless of latency, can predict maneuvers that have not yet occurred. The star fires into the entire maneuver envelope at once — physics requires the target to be somewhere inside it.

Star geometry

7-barrel baseline, adaptive spread

Arm separation is not fixed at manufacture. Mechanical actuators on each arm let FLP dynamically reshape the spread per engagement — narrow for an FPV drone, wide for an HGV maneuvering at 5 g.

Maneuver envelope

Coverage scales with target capability

For a target at velocity v, maximum lateral acceleration a_max and round flight time t_flight, the maneuver radius is R = ½ × a_max × t_flight². FLP computes the full 3D envelope as an ellipsoid from track data and optimizes arm placement against it — not against a symmetric circle.

Pillar 03 — Latency

ALTA Fiber Loop Photonics fire control

HKDS fire control is derived from the ALTA Fiber Loop Photonics architecture developed for SmallSat autonomy. Fiber Bragg gratings feed a photonic reservoir computer over an optical bus. Computation happens as interference in the photonic medium — no clock cycles, no analogue-to-digital conversion, no pipeline stages.

FLP architecture

Same computation, different application

The four-stage ALTA architecture maps directly onto fire-control requirements. The FLP isn't being repurposed — it's running the same time-series pattern-recognition task in a different physical domain.
