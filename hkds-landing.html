Concept

A third path between guns and missiles

Today, air defenses either fire slow conventional guns or expensive guided missiles. HKDS proposes a third category: hypervelocity kinetic rounds guided by photonic fire control, delivering missile-class terminal performance with gun-like simplicity and cost.

System overview

Distributed, autonomous, defensive battery

HKDS surrounds a defended site with corner-mounted turret nodes and a 10+ km sensor perimeter. A central Fiber Loop Photonics (FLP) node ingests radar, optical, and fiber sensor data, computes lead solutions via photonic reservoir computing, and pushes fire/no-fire commands back to the turrets in under a millisecond.

  • Fixed-site only

    Installed as a permanent or semi-permanent area-denial shield around bases and critical assets.

  • Multi-threat coverage

    Designed for FPV drones, loitering munitions, cruise missiles, hypersonic glide vehicles, and terminal ballistic threats.

  • Photonic intelligence core

    FLP-based fire control continuously processes time-series sensor data and issues firing decisions with sub-millisecond latency.

Threat spectrum

From hobby drones to hypersonic glide vehicles

HKDS is specified against a tiered threat spectrum, from low-cost FPV drones with minutes of warning time to hypersonic and ballistic terminal threats where the window shrinks to a few seconds.

Lower-tier threats

FPV drones and loitering munitions approach at tens to hundreds of meters per second, with tens to hundreds of seconds of warning at 10 km. HKDS can engage these cost-effectively with smaller rounds and hybrid conventional layers, preserving hypervelocity capacity for harder targets.

Cruise missiles

Subsonic and supersonic cruise missiles reduce warning times to roughly 10–40 seconds at 10 km. Conventional guns struggle with lead error and fire-control latency in this regime; HKDS combines higher projectile velocity with photonic lead calculation to keep intercept windows open.

Hypersonic & ballistic terminal

Hypersonic cruise, hypersonic glide vehicles, and ballistic reentry vehicles can compress warning time to 1.4–6.7 seconds. HKDS is optimized for this regime: hypervelocity kinetics cut required lead distance and FLP fire control delivers multiple decision cycles inside the terminal trajectory.

Technology pillars

Hypervelocity kinetics, belt-fed rounds, photonic fire control

HKDS integrates three core innovations: helium light gas guns for velocity, star-configuration multi-barrel turrets for maneuver coverage, and FLP photonic fire control for latency.

Light gas gun

Helium-propelled hypervelocity

A two-stage, electrically-driven light gas gun uses helium compressed to hundreds of megapascals to accelerate slender tungsten penetrators to 4–8 km/s. Helium offers a safe, inert propellant with an acoustic velocity high enough to reach the velocities needed for hypersonic intercepts, without the operational risks of hydrogen.

Star turret & belt feed

Multi-barrel spread, self-contained rounds

The turret is a star of launch tubes around a central axis. When commanded, multiple barrels fire simultaneously in a controlled spread, covering the full maneuver envelope of the target instead of betting on a single perfect intercept point.

Rounds are belt-fed, self-contained units with a pre-pressurized helium capsule, a sacrificial polymer sabot that protects the barrel, and a dense tungsten penetrator. Cyclic rates of 20–40 rounds per minute per barrel are achievable with electrically reset pistons.

Photonic fire control

Fiber Loop Photonics (FLP) fire-control core

HKDS uses a photonic fire-control module derived from the ALTA Fiber Loop Photonics architecture. Sensor data from the perimeter is encoded onto light and processed through a photonic reservoir computer, which excels at time-series prediction and anomaly detection.

Recent integrated next-generation reservoir computing engines demonstrate over 100 TOPS per square millimetre at tens of gigabaud, with energy scaling that is effectively O(1) with respect to reservoir size. Against that backdrop, HKDS's <1 ms fire-control requirement is conservative, not optimistic.

The end result: sensor-to-fire commands in sub-millisecond cycles, turning seconds of warning time into dozens of full decision loops.

Doctrine

Defensive only, by architecture and by intent

HKDS is explicitly designed as a defensive, terminal area-denial system. Its geometry, emplacement, and engagement logic all enforce a defensive role rather than offensive power projection.

What HKDS is

A fixed, autonomous, multi-threat terminal defense layer that engages incoming aerial objects only after they enter a defined defended airspace. It is an anti-access / area-denial shield that protects bases, cities, and critical infrastructure from above.

  • Fixed-site architecture

    Corner-mounted turrets and buried sensor rings tie HKDS to the asset it protects; it is not a mobile artillery piece.

  • Incoming-only engagement

    Rules of engagement restrict HKDS to positively identified, inbound aerial threats within the defended volume.

  • Purely kinetic payloads

    Rounds have no explosive warheads or guidance seekers; they are simple tungsten rods driven to hypervelocity.

Development path

From concept to range-proven battery

HKDS is designed as a co-development partner to ALTA's FLP program. The same photonic core that enables spacecraft autonomy can underpin terminal hypersonic defense on the ground.

Phase 1 — FLP ground demonstrator
Lab / hardware-in-the-loop
Build and exercise an FLP fire-control prototype against synthetic and recorded threat trajectories. Demonstrate end-to-end sensor-to-fire loop latency under one millisecond in a ground environment.
Phase 2 — LGG & turret prototype
Proving ground
Develop a single-barrel light gas gun and star-configuration turret demonstrator. Conduct live-fire tests against representative aerial surrogates to validate muzzle velocity, barrel life, and spread patterns.
Phase 3 — Integrated node
Full-stack node at test range
Integrate perimeter sensors, FLP node, and a turret into a single HKDS node defending a sector of a test range. Exercise the full chain under mixed-threat scenarios.
Phase 4 — Operational battery
Layered national defense architecture
Deploy a multi-node HKDS battery as the terminal layer in a layered air defense stack, complementing long-range missile interceptors and conventional short-range systems.
